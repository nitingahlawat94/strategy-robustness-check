Six ways your backtest can lie, checked.

Most strategies look great in a backtest. These checks show how much of that survives contact with reality.

Monte Carlo drawdown

Reshuffles your trades 2,000 times to show the drawdown you should plan for, not the lucky one in your backtest.

Luck test

Flips trade signs at random to measure how often pure chance matches your profit.

Deflated Sharpe

Tell it how many variants you tried. It shows how much of your edge survives that search.

Cost stress

Adds slippage and fees until the profit disappears, so you know your margin of safety.

Consistency

First half against second half, and five time windows. Edges that fade show up here.

Walk-forward

Compare in-sample and out-of-sample trades, or run a full walk-forward on price data.

Questions.

Is my data uploaded?

No. The analysis runs in your browser. Your file is read on your device and is not sent to a server.

Does a high score mean I will make money?

No. It means the trade list you gave us shows no major red flags. Markets change, and past trades never guarantee future results. This is not investment advice.

Which files work?

MetaTrader 4 and 5 statements, TradingView trade lists, and many broker P&L reports and tradebooks. If we cannot find the profit column, you can choose it yourself.

What is a Report ID?

A short code made from your trades, your settings and the tool version. It also seeds the simulations, so the same inputs always give the same results. It proves a result is reproducible, not that the trades are real.

Is it free?

Yes, today. Features that need servers, such as saved reports, sharing and automatic import, will be paid.
